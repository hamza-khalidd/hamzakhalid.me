Notes from the work.

Hamza Khalid writes from London about analytics, the pipelines under the chart, and what happens when a number has to survive a room.

24 Oct 2026

Response time is a data problem

On a fuel network, a slow report is not a formatting issue. It is stock, leakage, and money sitting in the wrong place. The Power BI work I care about is the gap between a bad signal and someone acting on it.

Across thousands of stations that gap is the product. Inventory, early leak signals, and loss prevention only help if the person who can do something sees them in time. A prettier chart that arrives late is still late.

24 Oct 2026

Fix the pipeline before the model

At EY the gains I still trust were unglamorous. ETL that could move three million records without falling over. Processing time that dropped because the path was simpler. Forecast accuracy moved after the data was fit to be forecast, not before.

Forty-plus BI projects will teach you the same sequence if you let them. Define the grain. Cut the path. Then model. The model is not a substitute for a pipeline that already lies.

24 Oct 2026

Put the number in the room

A dashboard that never meets a sceptical stakeholder is a file. I have presented BI work to officials, directors, and team heads, and some of it was shown at forums including COP28.

The preparation that mattered was the definition: what the figure includes, what it ignores, and which decision it is allowed to support. If you cannot say that out loud, the slide is not ready.

24 Oct 2026

When the title overclaims

A video title is a promise. The speech is the evidence. I built a check that takes a YouTube video from audio to text and scores how close that text sits to the title.

It is a small research project, not a platform. The useful part is the habit: do not trust the label until you have looked at the thing it claims to describe. The code is on GitHub.

24 Oct 2026

Package the step you repeat

Most analytics projects restart the same helpers: cleaning, a few transforms, the plot you always end up wanting. EtchKay is the module I kept instead of pasting that code again.

It is deliberately small. Version 0.0.1 is the first cut of a shorter path through data science and analytics work, published on PyPI and GitHub.

24 Oct 2026

Ordinary tools, used hard

The stack I keep coming back to is not exotic. Python, SQL, Power BI, Tableau, Azure. The work changes. The need to show a number someone will trust does not.

Specialism has been the reporting layer: DAX, SSRS, Power Automate, and the conversation with the person who has to use the figure. Tools are the easy part to list. The hard part is the definition underneath them.
